<h1>CYM 100: Research Methods for Cyber Security</h1>
<p>
In this introductory module, I supported students in developing the foundational academic and research skills required for their Msc cyber security thesis. This included guiding them through the literature review process, qualitative and quantitative data collection, and effective academic writing. I also provided support during practical workshops focused on research design, helping students structure research questions, evaluate the reliability of sources, and understand ethical considerations in cyber security related research.
</p>

<h1>IY3840: Malware Analysis</h1>
<p>
I assisted in the creation, configuration, and maintenance of virtual machine environments used for hands-on malware analysis and reverse-engineering exercises. My responsibilities included co-running the practical lab sessions alongside other TAs, supporting students as they explored static and dynamic analysis techniques, and demonstrating key tools such as disassemblers, debuggers, and sandboxing environments. I also contributed to developing example cases and walkthroughs to help students understand real-world malware behaviour.
</p>

<h1>IY5610/4610: Security Theory and Pentesting</h1>
<p>
For this module, I assisted in updating, testing, and maintaining the VM-based lab environment used for penetration-testing exercises and demonstrations. This involved preparing vulnerable systems, verifying that practical tasks worked as intended, and ensuring that lab environments were safe, stable, and isolated. I supported students during the practical sessions by helping them understand core security concepts, navigate common pentesting tools and frameworks, and apply theoretical knowledge to real-world attack scenarios. I also collaborated with the teaching team to refine lab content based on student feedback and evolving security practices.
</p>
